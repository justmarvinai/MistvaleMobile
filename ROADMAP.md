# Mistvale — Development Roadmap to EA-0.1

> The build plan for the game repo; the Admin Suite advances in lockstep (see `MistvaleMobile-Admin/ROADMAP.md`, phases A0–A6). Phases are **dependency-ordered, no skeletons**: each phase ships production-quality, tested, documented work — later phases never revisit "temporary" versions of earlier ones.
>
> **Checkpoint rule:** at the end of every phase I ask the owner whether there's feedback, bugs, or changes wanted before continuing — an *offer*, never a requirement; silence = continue. Open design decisions live in `USER_QUESTIONS.md`; each phase lists which pending answers it can absorb before its start.

**Progress, unlocks and the Depths (P6a–P6b):** stage progress — clears, stars kept at their best, first-clear bonuses and chapter star chests paid once, and the unlock chain that had been authored since P1 finally *enforced*, with the map greying out exactly what the server refuses · **the four unimplemented boss behaviours**, which the content schema had promised since P1 and the engine had ignored: the Ashpriest's persistent hit-counter shield (break it and it forfeits a turn; leave it and the team loses meter), the Rimebound Sentinel's per-band retaliation, the Broodmother's add-summoning, and the enrage ramp every boss now carries · a `dungeon` content type and **120 floors** across four relic keeps, the Proving Grounds and five Essence Springs, generated from ten plan entries · the springs rotation — Pure daily, each breath two days, Mist on Sunday — read against the operator's own reset hour and timezone, with every spring open during a new account's first week · drops by floor band, including the Silkmire's accessories below floor 10 · the Depths hub and floor picker · balance-sim gates per dungeon: entry, deepest, and *wall* (the deepest floor must turn back an entry-level team, or the ladder is decoration). 17 Depths cases, 14 boss-mechanic cases, and the whole suite at 528.

**Masteries (P6c):** the 48 nodes across three trees, with the 2-of-3 tree rule and the fifteen-pick budget evaluated by one shared helper the tree screen and the server both call · twenty-one engine effect types covering every node, so a mastery is content and only a new *kind* of effect is a deploy · the split that keeps the champion screen honest — unconditional stats folded in before the fight, alongside relics and in their own column; conditions and procs evaluated in the battle · Deathmark, Last Bastion and Veilbinder behaving as their text says · tier prices in `game_config`, spent through the Proving Grounds' emblems, with a first free reset per champion. 15 engine cases and 17 server cases; the whole suite at 562.

**Multi-battle & the sandbox (P6d):** farming without watching — N runs of one stage resolved server-side under a single player lock, each with its own fresh seed, paying exactly what the same runs would have paid by hand · the count trimmed by the server to the smallest of what was asked, the per-press cap, today's allowance and what energy covers, with the summary naming which one bit, and a lost run ending the batch while keeping what the earlier ones earned · **no session rows per run** — thirty states and thirty logs is megabytes per farm and a batch has nothing to resume, so the summary *is* the record, kept on the player row where a retried request replays it instead of farming twice · a daily-allowance mechanism with no reset job: one counter map stamped with the game-day, so a stale stamp reads as zero and an account away for a month is current the moment it returns (the shape every P8 quest counter will use) · `lib/game-day`, now shared by the springs rotation and the allowance, so "today" is one answer · the practice sandbox — any stage already cleared, re-fought for no energy and no reward at all, stars still reported because that is the question it answers · the team-select screen grown into all three ways to fight a stage. 12 server cases, 10 game-day cases and a browser run; the whole suite at 584.

**The whole campaign (P6e):** twelve chapters on three difficulties — **252 stages**, generated from twelve plan entries, so a thirteenth chapter is one entry and a publish · twelve named warlords, each with a signature of its own, and four of them carrying a keep-boss mechanic in a gentler form so the Depths teaches nothing from scratch: Hissrad's six-hit ward, Mama Fenwrack's per-eighth retaliation, Ryssa's twelve-hit ward behind a Reflect, and the Coilmother's brood · wave width and a per-chapter theme archetype, so a late stage is harder than an early one at the same level rather than merely longer · the star turn limit now grows with the chapter, because a fixed one quietly put the third star out of reach of the players who earned it · **a difficulty opens on finishing the whole one below it** (source-faithful), which is what lets the level bands assume a levelled account instead of hedging · star chests at 7/21/42/63 counting all three difficulties together · four new balance gates including two *walls* — 12-7 Normal turns back a chapter-1 team and Brutal 12-7 turns back a team fresh off Normal — without which a rebalance could flatten twelve chapters into one and nothing would notice · the campaign map folded into chapters, opening on the one you are actually in. 14 structural cases over the generated ladder and a browser run of the folded map; the whole suite at 598.

### P8 — Meta & retention
ProgressService fan-out + quest/mission/event/tutorial goal tracking. Daily/weekly/monthly quests + chest + **daily first-win-of-mode bonuses**; Missions (80 arcs, finale: exclusive Legendary Aureleth); Events framework + 3 presets + admin scheduling; login calendar + welcome track; mailbox + admin mail composer; news feed; player profile card + settings (audio/speed/motion/credits/password); daily reset job wired to everything. Admin reaches A4/A5 completeness.
**Exit:** a day in Mistvale has structure: log in → calendar → quests → energy plan → events — the retention layer breathes.
**Delivered so far (P8a — the goal engine):** the goal DSL — `{type, target, filters}` over a registry of twenty report types, with accumulation (`count` vs `highest`) a property of the type rather than of each goal, and publish validation that refuses a filter its type does not declare 〔`{type:'summon', mode:'campaign'}` looks reasonable in the editor and would silently never complete〕 · `quest` as an eighteenth content type, so a new daily is an Admin edit rather than a deploy, with the 19 seeds of ECONOMY §11 〔8 dailies across eight *different* systems, 6 weeklies, 5 monthlies〕 · `player_quests` / `player_missions` / `player_events` / `login_claims`, the quest instance keyed by a **period anchor** so "today's dailies" is a lookup and there is no job that deletes yesterday · **`ProgressService.track`** — one fan-out, typed to accept only a transaction, locking the player row so a battle settling while a purchase lands cannot lose one of the two 〔pinned by a test that records 6 instead of 24 without it〕 · battle settle and the arena reporting through it, and nothing in either knowing what a quest is. **37 goal-engine tests**; 774 green overall.
**Delivered so far (P8b — the checklist):** `GET /quests` answering the whole screen in one read 〔three tabs and three meters are still one state, and answering it three times is how two panels disagree about whether the day has turned over〕 · claim and chest-claim, both idempotent — a retried claim replays what it paid, a second one is refused — both returning the whole screen again so nothing renders one claim stale · the **completion chest**, which counts quests *claimed* rather than merely finished, keyed by a period anchor on the player row so it re-opens at the reset with nothing to reset it, and which is where `claimAllDailies` is reported from · **first win of the day per mode**, paid automatically on the win with no claim, on the same stamped daily-counter map as every other allowance · the Quests screen, the `Rewards` primitive every reward map now renders through, and the dock pip computed on the snapshot the shell already re-fetches · **a reward map that pays what it says** — `payRewards` splits currencies from items and publish validation resolves every item key, closing a silent drop that would have let a first-clear reward promise a sigil and hand over nothing · **`SEED.sh` fills in what an install is missing** 〔found by the browser suite, not by a test: a release's new content type and its `game_config` keys could never reach a live server without `--force-content` discarding the operator's tuning — the dev database turned out to be 21 config rows behind, silently running on code fallbacks〕. 27 new tests and 4 browser cases; 805 green overall.
**Delivered so far (P8c — the Valewarden's Path):** **eighty missions in ten arcs of eight**, `mission` as a nineteenth content type, ending in **Aureleth, Voice of the Vale** — an exclusive Legendary the Mistgate will never roll — and the title "Warden of the Reclamation" · arcs open in order and the eight inside one are open together 〔strictly sequential is a wall the day somebody cannot do step 43; an open list is not a path〕 · **progress accrues on every arc, claiming is what the gate controls**, so farming hard early leaves later arcs part-done rather than starting them at zero · the mission screen, the arc rail, the granted-champion callout and a second dock pip · publish validation over goal filters, reward items, granted champions, duplicate steps and gaps in the arc numbering. **Plus the fix P8b needed:** four of the eight dailies asked for events *no module reported* — summon, champion level-up, relic upgrade and Bazaar purchase — so they could never complete and the daily chest could never open; every remaining reporter is wired, and a test now asserts that no shipped quest asks for an event nothing sends. 22 new tests and 4 browser cases; 821 green overall.
**Delivered so far (P8d — timed events):** the framework — `event` as a twentieth content type whose point rules *are* goals with a rate attached, so an event can count anything a quest can and a new report type serves both at once · **three presets**, Champion Training (Mon–Fri), Depths Delve (Fri–Sun) and Summon Surge (Sat–Sun, source-faithful sigil weights), six-rung ladders each · **no scheduler** — a window is derived from the clock like energy and arena tokens, so a server down all weekend comes back with the right events live and nothing to catch up on · **recurring rather than the planned two-week absolute calendar** 〔a calendar that must be re-cut by hand every fortnight is one that stops being cut; the absolute form is still there for a one-off〕 · occurrences, so next week's ladder starts over with nothing to reset · a claim grace, so a ladder finished on Sunday evening is still collectable on Monday · the events screen, its ladder track, and a third dock pip. 26 new tests and 4 browser cases; 847 green overall.

**Delivered so far (P8e — the login calendar):** two tracks — the **thirty-day calendar** that cycles forever and the **seven-day welcome strip** a newcomer walks once — as `loginTrack`, a twenty-first content type holding one entity per track rather than per day 〔a track is only ever read whole, and "re-cut August's calendar" should be one draft to review, not thirty〕 · **a day is paid on the Nth claim, not the Nth of the month**, so missing a Tuesday loses that Tuesday and not the player's place — and the whole of a track's state is then `count(*)` over its claims plus "was one of them today", so there is nothing for the daily reset to do and no counter that can drift from the ledger under it · **the day-30 Epic selector** — a choice of four non-starter Epics, one per role, granted inside the claim's transaction · the welcome track's closing **relic set**, four Ironroot and two Swiftwind, which teaches that sets stack and that two can be worn at once · the calendar screen, its thirty-tile grid and seven-tile strip, the single glowing tile, the selector dialog and a fourth dock pip · publish validation over day gaps and duplicates, a second active track of a kind, reward items, granted and offered champions, and relic sets · a server-side gate at level 2 (`unlocks.loginCalendarLevel`) that costs a late arrival nothing, since their first claim is still day one. 25 new tests and 4 browser cases; 872 green overall.

**Delivered so far (P8f — mail and news):** the **mailbox** — a row per message per player, attachments paid through `RewardService` so a gift lands in `economy_log` beside every other grant, collect-all as *one* transaction and one ledger row rather than twenty, and a claim that replays on a retry and is refused on a second 〔the one surface where an operator hands out currency directly is the one that must never pay twice〕 · **expiry derived, never swept** — an expired message is a `where` clause, so a server down for a week comes back with the right inbox and the daily prune is only about disk · **the composer** — one player or everybody, fanning out inside one transaction so a send reaches all or none, never reaching a bot, with attachments validated against the live item catalogue *before* anything is written 〔there is no publish step between an operator typing `sigil_gleeming` and a thousand players opening a message that pays nothing〕, audited, and stamped with the operator's own account name so a player can ask about it · the **send log**, grouped by batch: reached / read / collected, which is the question a compensation mail actually raises · **news as `newsPost`**, a twenty-second content type with a window — a post written Tuesday and published once appears by itself on Friday, the same derive-don't-tick the events use — and a seeded welcome post plus an inactive patch-note template · the **`Prose` renderer**, which turns markdown-lite into React elements and never into HTML, because a feed is the most broadcast surface the game has · the mail screen, the news dialog, and the top bar's one pip · and **`RewardPicker`** in the Admin suite, built as the shared primitive the quest, mission, event and login-track editors will want rather than inside the composer. 22 new tests and 4 browser cases; 894 green overall.

**Delivered so far (P8g — the public card):** the **profile card** GAME_DESIGN §142 asked for — name, title, level, arena tier with ladder position, champions owned against the collectable roster, the furthest campaign stage phrased the way a player says it 〔"7-4 Hard", and Hard 1-1 outranks Normal 12-7 because it is the wall that gates everything after it〕, total stars, and the four champions the account chose to be known by · **what the card leaves out is the point of it**: no wallet, no account name, and nothing that marks an account as a bot — pinned by a test that fails if any of the three ever appears · **a showcase that is never empty** — a player who has not chosen gets their strongest, so the picker is something to reach for rather than something to get past, and a champion released after being chosen drops out rather than leaving a hole · reachable from the three places a name appears: the top-bar chip for your own, and the Arena's ladder rows and opponent offers for anybody else's, both of which now carry the player id a card is fetched by. **Plus the two the screens' own pass turned up:** the Haven built its camp from a deny-list rather than the dock's predicate and had quietly adopted `battle`, a tile that walked into a battle screen with no battle; and `useLiveEnergy` had been handing `Date.now` to `useSyncExternalStore` as a snapshot — a getSnapshot with no fixed point, latent since P0 and surfaced the moment the top bar gained a second subscription. 17 new tests and 3 browser cases; 913 green overall.

**Delivered (P8i — the nightly pass, and what it must never touch):** the **prune** — resolved battles with the event logs they carry, expired mail past a grace, the economy trail, quest instances whose period is long past, event scores whose occurrence is 〔quest rows are the volume: eight a day per active account, forever〕 · every window a `game_config` key, because "how long do we keep battle logs" is a decision an operator makes on a box that is filling up, not one compiled into a release · **`NEVER_PRUNED`, enforced rather than commented** — `login_claims` above all, whose `count(*)` *is* the login calendar's position, so a prune that swept it would silently walk every player backwards through the month; the test runs with every window at zero, the harshest setting the config permits, and fails if any guarded table loses a row · an **active battle is never pruned at any setting**, because somebody who left a fight open over a weekend comes back to it · `POST /jobs/run/:name` and its list, a **closed map of two** rather than a name that reaches anything callable, audited, for the operator who has just shortened a window or published content the bot ladder should pick up · and the property the whole phase rests on written down at last: **the daily job resets nothing.** Energy, tokens, quest periods, event windows, mail expiry and the calendar are all derived on read, so a missed run costs disk and never state — and anything that would need it belongs on the read path instead. 11 new tests; 924 green overall.

### P10 — Release hardening & EA-0.1 launch
Full content seed review + balance-sim pass over everything; audio (CC0 SFX/music per ASSET_GUIDE, mixer + settings); animation/transition polish (screen wipes, reward bursts, Haven ambience); performance audit (bundle split, atlas sizes, API p95, DB indexes, RAM ceilings); security pass (rate limits, fuzz inputs, session hygiene, admin allowlist); PWA manifest + installability; Playwright E2E smoke; DEPLOY/UPDATE/BACKUP/RESTORE drill on a scratch VPS + real VPS deploy; content export→git snapshot; `ea-0.1.0` tag; launch checklist + day-1 monitoring.
**Exit:** **EA-0.1 live** on the VPS, friends onboarded, backups proven, dashboard quiet.

**Delivered (P10a — the two clocks):** the hardening pass opened by measuring the cold open and finding it three seconds long. It was not: **the results modal was keyed on the server's response rather than on the playback**, so it opened on top of a HUD reading "Turn 0" and every fight in the game gave its outcome away before it was watched — including the near-death beat tuned and gated in P9b, which nobody had ever seen. The two clocks a battle runs on are now named and separated: **commands read the server** (Auto and Retreat go dark when the session closes), **outcomes read the playback** (results, the closing line, and the wallet — silver climbing at turn three announced a win as plainly as the modal did). Skip is the deliberate way past a fight, and the bar now distinguishes "the server is thinking" from "this is a recording, and here is the button". Also fixed on the way past: retreating mid-playback re-applied the whole event log on top of a view that already held part of it. 6 unit cases; the browser suite presses Skip like a player in a hurry, and `tutorial.spec.ts` gained the regression itself — the fight must be visibly under way with the outcome still hidden.

**Delivered (P10b — one arbiter for what is on top):** the layering pass the note below asked for, and it turned out not to be about z-index at all — the tokens were fine. It was that **nothing arbitrated which of two open dialogs was live**. Both installed a capture-phase key listener on `document`, and `stopPropagation` does nothing about a sibling listener on the same node, so **Escape closed both** (reachable today: the relic picker over the champion sheet) and Tab wandered out of the top dialog into the trap still running underneath it. Overlays now register on open, the last one in owns the keyboard and the backdrop, and its depth is added to `$z-modal` so the stacking order is the order things were opened. The Mistgate's reveal joins the same stack. Focus follows the dialog rather than the stack — and stopped being re-grabbed on every re-render of the screen underneath, which it had been doing because the trap's dependencies included an inline `onClose`. 8 unit cases and a browser case that presses Escape with two dialogs open.

**Delivered (P10d — the pass, and what it turned up):** the perf half found nothing to fix and said so with numbers — every budget in ARCHITECTURE §9 now carries a measured figure and all of them are an order of magnitude inside (6–22 ms p95 on the hot reads against a 100 ms target, 199 MB of Node against 1.2 GB, 302 KB of gzipped JS against 1.5 MB, 80 KB of content bundle against 500 KB). Two notes kept rather than fixed: the bundle is only small **because nginx compresses it** — the origin emits 708 KB — and the sequential scans on `stage_progress` are the planner being right at fourteen rows, not a missing index.
The security half found two real ones — and chasing a flaky browser test turned up a third, in the economy. **A mistyped id answered 500**: seven routes handed a path parameter straight to a `uuid` column, where PostgreSQL's 22P02 became "Something went wrong on our end" — on paths reachable from every player name in the game, so a stale link told the player the game was down and paged whoever was on call. Path and query are now parsed like bodies always were (`lib/params.ts`), a malformed id answers identically to a missing one so the shape of our keys stays private, page sizes are clamped rather than trusted, and the error handler maps 22P02 as a backstop for routes written later. **And the global rate limiter never bucketed per account**: at Fastify's default `onRequest` hook the session has not been resolved, so everyone behind one address shared 300 requests a minute. Both are pinned by tests that fail without the fix — including a sweep that walks every parameterised route with a garbage id.

**And the third:** `management.spec.ts` had been failing about one run in fifteen, always at "no relic in five clears". Rather than bump the retry, the drop stream was measured — 200,000 battle seeds through the real loot RNG pay out **42.12%** against a configured 42%, and five dry runs in a row happen to 6.54% of accounts against a theoretical 6.56%. The drop table and the RNG are both exactly right; the test was staking a P4 exit criterion on a coin, and its retry is now sized against the measured odds rather than a guess. But the same look asked why the loop only ever affords five runs, and the answer was a **level-up that never refilled the energy bar** — documented in ECONOMY_BALANCE since P0 as one of the three sources of energy, and simply not implemented: `RewardService.grant` wrote the new level and left the column alone. Nine phases of nobody asking. Fixed, with the overfill rule kept (a level must not trim a bar a refill item pushed past its cap) and six cases, three of which fail without it.

**Delivered (P10c — the game makes a noise):** the volume sliders had been on screen since P8 connected to nothing. **`soundCue` is the twenty-fourth content type** — a key, a bus, and either a recording or a handful of envelope-and-oscillator numbers — so what the game sounds like is retunable in Admin without a deploy and a dropped-in pack replaces a synth voice one field at a time. Twenty-seven cues ship, **synthesised rather than sampled**: this environment cannot reach Kenney or OpenGameArt, and inert sliders were the one option the brief rules out. Short shaped tones and filtered noise, which is what a pixel game's interface has always been; nothing to licence, credit or download. **Q4 answered 2026-08-18: synthesised stays the voice at EA**, with a CC0 pack a drop-in whenever one is picked — content, not code. The vocabulary is deliberately about twenty-five meanings rather than one per control. A press is a property of `Button` and `Modal` rather than of screens, so a screen cannot forget; battle cues ride the **playback** clock like everything else that gives a fight away; nothing plays before the page has been touched, because a refused `AudioContext` stays refused; and a missing cue is silence rather than an error, since cues are content. Music is a bus with no track and the slider says so. 39 cases across the synth (pure: voice → samples), the mixer (a fake device counting what would have played) and the catalogue.

**Delivered (P10e — installable, snapshot-able, checked):** the PWA — a manifest, a worker and icons at every size, **exported from the one mark the repo already holds** (`pnpm icons:pwa`) rather than drawn again. The worker caches the shell network-first and the hashed assets cache-first and **never touches `/api`**: the server is authoritative, so a cached response would be a lie about an account somebody could act on. Production only, because a worker that claims the page in development eats Vite's modules and its HMR socket · **`pnpm content:export`** writes the live content into `content-snapshot/` as sorted, byte-stable JSON, so the game's balance and copy stop living only where `git log` cannot see them and an operator's evening becomes a reviewable diff · **`scripts/CHECK_DEPLOY.sh`, wired into CI** — every operations script parsed and shellchecked, the nginx site rendered and handed to nginx's own parser, and every template placeholder checked against the ones `render_template` substitutes; the least-exercised code in the repository is now checked on every push instead of first thing on a deploy · and **`docs/LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md`**, including the step everybody skips: restore a backup before there are players, while the only account it can lose is a throwaway.

**Delivered (P10f — the transitions, and a setting that meant nothing):** the **mist wipe** UI_UX has specified since P0 and nobody had built — 200 ms on every navigation, drawn over a navigation that has already happened rather than gating one, so pressing two dock tiles quickly is as fast as it ever was. **DOM rather than the Pixi overlay first imagined**: a full-screen gradient is one composited layer the GPU handles for free, where Pixi would contend with the battle ticker for the box's single core, and `prefers-reduced-motion` switches it off with nothing in JavaScript asking. And **Reduce motion stopped being decoration** — in Settings since P8, writing to the server and changing nothing on screen, because the stylesheet honoured only the *operating system's* preference; a player who wanted a calmer interface had to change it for their whole machine. Both answers are honoured identically now, and sprite idles are untouched by either, since those are the game being alive rather than the interface being busy.

**Delivered (the QA pass — what a full read of the game found):** the owner asked for a quality check before anything else, ranked, and then for it to be fixed. Fourteen findings; the headline is that **the battle screen had been rendering nothing at all**, and the reason it survived ten phases is the thread running through most of the rest: *nothing in this repository could see what the game looked like.* `BattleScreen` mounted a second `<PixiStage>` — `position: fixed; inset: 0`, opaque, later in the DOM at the same layer — straight over the HUD, the turn order, ×1/Skip/Auto/Retreat and the whole skill bar. Forty browser tests drove it through roles and text and every one of them passed. `BattleScene` also had no `resize`, so the fight drew at its 960×540 design size in a corner of the window; nobody had noticed, because the screen was never visible.

**And 34 of 37 champions had no face.** Only three of the eight sprite units ship an `avatar.png`; `ChampionCard` rendered a bare `<img>` with no `onError`, so the roster, the Chronicle and the card a Mistgate pull turns over were full of the browser's torn-page glyph. A portrait is now a loaded image or a hooded silhouette from game-icons.net and never a broken one — and the dock's seventeen hand-typed emoji went the same way, since the brief has said icons come from game-icons.net since P0 and navigation was the last place drawing its own.

**Then the ones that hid the rest.** There was **no error boundary anywhere**, over sixteen screens whose content comes from a database an operator edits live — so one malformed entity reaching a render path took the whole game to a white page. **A missing file answered 200 and handed back the game**: `try_files $uri $uri/ /index.html` is right for a route and a lie about a file, so a dropped sprite came back as an HTML document, `fetch(…manifest.json)` failed with a parse error about `<`, and nothing in the network tab said *missing*. **Signing out forgot three stores out of eighteen**, so signing back in as somebody else on the same page load painted the previous player's roster, their mail and their fight. **The playback clock outlived its screen**, playing hit cues over the sign-in form. **A retried start was told it was already in a battle** — about a fight it could not see and had paid for. **The mailbox had no ceiling**, and the unread pip hydrated every message to count them. **A failed texture load ended in `.catch(() => null)`**, and one rejected manifest promise poisoned the latch for the rest of the session.

**The coverage that would have caught the first two on their own**, because a fix without one is a promise: `e2e/visible.spec.ts` asserts that the things a player must be able to see are the topmost elements at their own centres — `document.elementFromPoint`, not pixel comparison, which fails on a font hint and teaches everyone to ignore it — that the page holds exactly one canvas, that no image is broken and that the icon sprite is inlined. `e2e/resilience.spec.ts` breaks a screen from outside the app and watches the frame stay standing. And `CHECK_DEPLOY.sh` **starts nginx** against a tree of fixture files and asks it twenty-one questions, because "the site parses" is a much weaker claim than it reads as: this config parsed perfectly for ten phases while doing the wrong thing.

**One finding is deliberately not fixed:** the relic vault has no cap and `GET /player/gear` returns all of it. A cap is what makes selling and dismantling matter, which makes it a design decision rather than a bug — it is **Q5** in `USER_QUESTIONS.md` with a recommended default (500, in `game_config`), not a change made on the owner's behalf.

**Delivered (the owner's box, second pass — and the worst bug of the whole QA sweep):** two
reports against the running game, and the first one is that **manual play had never worked**.

**Delivered (Q5 — the relic vault has a ceiling):** answered by the owner on 2026-08-18, and

### D — The design rework (in progress, commissioned 2026-08-18)

*"I want you to FULLY rework the Design of the whole Game."* The game is dressed in
**FantasyUIs** — the owner's own vanilla-TS component library — vendored into
`apps/client/src/fui/` rather than depended on. This supersedes the hand-built pixel kit and
the "no component library" rule; everything else in the brief stands. **Gameplay and content
are untouched by all of it**: this pass changes what the game looks like and nothing about
what it does.

The rule the phases are decided by, arrived at in D4 and applied since: **the library owns
chrome, React owns behaviour.** Where the library's component *is* the semantic element and
holds no state React must drive, the component is used. Where React owns the state, the art
is used and the element stays ours — which is why the mastery board and the four-column stat
table deliberately did not move.
